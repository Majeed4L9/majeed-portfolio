Neither of my current roles puts me next to a senior product designer day to day. I want to be taught properly: to see how an experienced designer makes decisions, to get honest feedback on my work, and to grow toward owning whole problems, as your listing describes.

Making twenty agents feel as calm as one is the kind of problem I want to spend my time on.

  • Self-directed work.Give me a problem and I'll take it from research to a clickable prototype on my own, and ask when I'm stuck.
  • A user's view of AI tools.I use Claude, ChatGPT and Lovable daily, so I quickly notice where an AI tool is hard to steer or hides what it is doing.

Questions I'd bring on day one

Which card do I open first?

Twenty running tickets is a priority problem before it is a layout problem. In Marlowe I learned that the nearest deadline is not always the most urgent item. What is the right signal on a board of agents?

What does a blocked agent need?

How fast can someone see that an autonomous cloud agent is stuck or blocked, and exactly what context it needs to resume? I want to master these interactive workflows before attempting to design them.

How do you review fast work?

Marlowe taught me that people trust AI output when they can check it. I'd like to learn what checking looks like for a pull request diff that an agent wrote in its own worktree.
